Our runs: Lost in Conversation, 103 GSM8K problems

Now all four assistants. The design is 2 by 2: CollabLLM's LoRA adapter, the small set of weights it trains on Llama, on or off, crossed with its system prompt on or off. The paper's own evaluation gives only CollabLLM its prompt, which is why we test the two separately. We also checked that the adapter is active: it favours CollabLLM's chosen replies in 72% of pairs (backup). Base first: 76.7 whole, 42.9 split. Click: CollabLLM, the adapter alone, drops just as much, 32 points. Click: Base with the prompt drops 30. Click: CollabLLM with the prompt drops 27, the least. Click: compared with Base, CollabLLM's drop is 1.5 points smaller, interval minus 5.4 to 8.3. CollabLLM plus prompt's is 7.0 smaller, interval 0.1 to 13.9: it only just excludes zero and is one of three comparisons, so it is suggestive.

What changes inside these conversations? Without the prompt, Base and CollabLLM behave alike: 7 to 8% of turns ask a question, and they attempt about 2.5 answers per conversation. The adapter only nudges final-answer accuracy, from 36.9 to 41.2%. Click: with CollabLLM's prompt, over a third of turns ask, answer attempts drop to about 1.6, and final answers are right 50 to 52% of the time. No surprise: the prompt literally says the assistant 'always asks for clarification rather than making assumptions' (backup 6). To sum up. When a math problem arrives in pieces, every assistant still drops 27 to 34 points. CollabLLM's training alone makes no clear difference; the asking we saw came from its prompt.
