What changes inside these conversations? Without the prompt, Base and CollabLLM behave alike: 7 to 8% of turns ask a question, and they attempt about 2.5 answers per conversation. The adapter learned CollabLLM's preferences but barely changes behaviour

0.06%median change to each weight matrix

Our analysis of CollabLLM's released math adapter and training data; Our runs: Lost in Conversation, 103 GSM8K problems

So why doesn't the adapter matter? First, it is tiny: the median weight matrix changes by 0.06%. Its learning rates, 1e-5 and 5e-6, suit full fine-tuning, while LoRA usually trains around 1e-4. Click: yet it learned. On 300 pairs from CollabLLM's released data, its DPO margin, how much more it favours the chosen reply than Base does, is above zero for 72%: mean plus 3.71 nats, interval 3.05 to 4.38. We can't confirm these were its exact training pairs. Click: but in Lost in Conversation it adds only 0.7 points of turns that ask, from 7.3 to 8.0%, and premature answers move by 1.4 points.

Our analysis of CollabLLM's released training data

Last, the training signal itself. As on method-3, each reply's score averages 3 simulated sessions, and method-5's pairs keep the higher score as chosen. In CollabLLM's math data, 60% of the 2,527 pairs are equally correct, so style and length alone decide; 40% differ in correctness. Click: the style-only pairs have small gaps, so rerunning the 3 sessions would flip about 18% of their labels, against 12%. Click: 46% have a gap within one standard error, the noise level of a 3-session average, against 32%. The signal meant to teach collaboration is the least reliable. These are normal-approximation sizes, not exact rates.

To sum up. In our test, when a math problem arrives in pieces, CollabLLM gets as lost as its base model. The collaboration we did see, asking first and answering right more often, came from its system prompt rather than its trained adapter. And the labels meant to teach collaboration are the noisiest.
