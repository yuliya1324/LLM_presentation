Two bugs in the benchmark's math scoring, both fixed by re-scoring every conversation. First, the answer extractor must copy answers verbatim, and GPT-4o-mini likely drops thousands separators: extraction failed for 33% of answers of 1,000 or more, against 1.3% below. After our fix it is 0.2 and 0.5%. Second, the evaluator trims cents with rstrip of dot-zero-zero, which strips characters rather than a suffix, so 720.00 became 72; we use removesuffix. Together, 115 conversations became correct and none became wrong. Every assistant still drops 26 to 34 points.

How we measured the adapter and the label noise

$\text{adapter size} = \operatorname{median} \dfrac{\lVert \Delta W \rVert_F}{\lVert W \rVert_F}, \qquad \Delta W = (\alpha / r)\, B A$
$\text{DPO margin} = \log \dfrac{\pi_{\text{CollabLLM}}(y^+)}{\pi_{\text{Base}}(y^+)} - \log \dfrac{\pi_{\text{CollabLLM}}(y^-)}{\pi_{\text{Base}}(y^-)}$
$\text{flip chance} = \Phi(-\text{gap} / \text{SE}), \qquad \text{SE} = \sqrt{(\sigma_+^2 + \sigma_-^2) / 2}$
  • $y^+, y^-$: chosen and rejected reply; $\sigma_\pm$: SD of their 3 session scores

Our analysis of CollabLLM's released math adapter and training data

The three formulas behind our adapter and label-noise numbers. Adapter size: for each of the 224 weight matrices the LoRA adapter changes, the norm of its change, alpha over r times B times A, relative to the original weight; we report the median. DPO margin: how much more the adapter raises the chosen reply's log-probability, y plus, than the rejected one's, y minus, compared with Base, in nats and in CollabLLM's training format. Above zero, it prefers the chosen reply more than Base does. Flip chance: the normal probability that 3-session noise reverses a pair; SE is the standard error of the difference of two 3-session means.

More issues in the released code and paper

Character-level BLEU

2

LoRA learning rates

1e-5 and 5e-6, typical of full fine-tuning; LoRA usually trains near 1e-4.

Corrected headline

Authors' IJCAI-26 version: 46.3% → 44.3%; Math +30.4%, not +36.4%.

examples/metrics/bleu.py, github.com/Wuyxin/collabllm; Table 6, p. 15; Table 1, p. 6; IJCAI-26 extended abstract (Wu et al., 2026)

Three more issues, for questions. First, the released code computes the writing benchmark's BLEU on characters, not words: nltk's sentence_bleu receives raw strings, so two paraphrased sentences that score 0.0 on word-level BLEU get 0.505. Second, Table 6's learning rates, 1e-5 for SFT and 5e-6 for DPO, are typical of full fine-tuning; LoRA is usually trained around 1e-4, which may explain how small the adapter's change is. Third, the authors themselves corrected the interactivity headline in their IJCAI-26 extended abstract: 44.3% instead of 46.3%, with Math at +30.4% rather than +36.4%.
