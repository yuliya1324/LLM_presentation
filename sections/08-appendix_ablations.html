Our runs: Lost in Conversation, 103 GSM8K problems

We ran everything ourselves. GPT-4o-mini plays the benchmark's lazy user and reveals one piece per turn. One vLLM server on a single RTX 3090 serves Llama-3.1-8B in bf16, with or without CollabLLM's math LoRA and its system prompt. GPT-4o-mini also labels every turn and extracts answers; we re-score them and bootstrap 95% intervals over the 103 problems. The 3,296 conversations took 2 h 54 min and about 3.35 dollars.

Two scoring bugs, both fixed by re-scoring every conversation. The extractor must copy answers verbatim, and GPT-4o-mini likely drops thousands separators: extraction failed for 33% of answers of 1,000 or more; after our fix, 0.2%. The evaluator's rstrip of dot-zero-zero turned 720.00 into 72; we use removesuffix. 115 conversations became correct, none became wrong, and every assistant still drops 26 to 34 points.

The prompt moves turns from answering to asking. Interrogation, several questions at once, rises from about 4 to 26% of turns, and answer attempts fall from 46 or 47 to 29 or 31%. The model answers about 1.6 instead of 2.5 times per conversation, fewer answers are premature, given before the last piece, and those are right more often. The adapter alone moves each share by under 2 points.

Proactive

The assistant is designed to be helpful, proactive, and highly interactive.

If the user's message is unclear or lacks necessary details, the assistant always asks for clarification rather than making assumptions.

Follow up

The assistant asks specific follow-up questions and offers suggestions based on the user's needs, avoiding vague or generic prompts.

No, the math adapter is not unusual. All three released adapters, math, code and writing, change the median weight matrix by 0.05 to 0.06% and none by more than about 0.1%, over the same 224 matrices; the largest changes sit in early value projections. On the math pairs, the adapter mostly lowered the rejected replies, a log-ratio of minus 3.52 against plus 0.19 for the chosen ones, so it learned mainly what to avoid.

Here are the exact numbers. Pairs with two equally correct replies have small score gaps, 0.132 on average, so 3-session noise would flip 17.6% of them, and 45.5% lie within one standard error. Pairs that differ in correctness have 0.592, 11.8% and 31.9%. The pairs offline DPO actually keeps are only slightly cleaner. How we measured the adapter and the label noise

$\text{adapter size} = \operatorname{median} \dfrac{\lVert \Delta W \rVert_F}{\lVert W \rVert_F}, \qquad \Delta W = (\alpha / r)\, B A$
$\text{DPO margin} = \log \dfrac{\pi_{\text{CollabLLM}}(y^+)}{\pi_{\text{Base}}(y^+)} - \log \dfrac{\pi_{\text{CollabLLM}}(y^-)}{\pi_{\text{Base}}(y^-)}$
$\text{flip chance} = \Phi(-\text{gap} / \text{SE}), \qquad \text{SE} = \sqrt{(\sigma_+^2 + \sigma_-^2) / 2}$
  • $y^+, y^-$: chosen and rejected reply; $\sigma_\pm$: population SD of their 3 session scores, so SE is the standard error of the difference of two 3-session means

Our analysis of CollabLLM's released math adapter and training data

Adapter size is each adapted matrix's change, alpha over r times B times A, relative to the weight itself, as a median over 224 matrices. The DPO margin is how much more the adapter raises the chosen reply, y plus, than the rejected one, y minus, compared with Base, in nats. The flip chance is the normal probability that 3-session noise reverses a pair, using the standard error of the difference of two 3-session means.

examples/metrics/bleu.py, github.com/Wuyxin/collabllm; Table 6, p. 15; Table 1, p. 6; IJCAI-26 extended abstract (Wu et al., 2026)

Three more, if asked. The released code computes the writing benchmark's BLEU on characters, so two paraphrases that score 0.0 on words get 0.505. The learning rates, 1e-5 for SFT and 5e-6 for DPO, are typical of full fine-tuning, while LoRA usually trains near 1e-4, which may explain the tiny adapter. And the authors themselves corrected the interactivity headline to 44.3% in their IJCAI-26 abstract.
